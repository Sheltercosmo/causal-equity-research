# Customer value, future industry states and turning points

Read for A02–A14, A18/A19 and causal-loop reviews as relevant. Implements v2 sections 5–13, 19–20 and 23; use sector-specific mechanisms, not one universal growth formula.

## Business value before financial extrapolation

For each customer job and cohort compare the candidate with the best feasible rival, internal solution, simpler product, deferment or no purchase. A08 independently reads the relevant full primary papers, technical specifications and test protocols. Distinguish matched measurements, vendor demonstrations, roadmaps and missing evidence. No claim of superiority from branding, generic AI excitement or one cherry-picked benchmark.

Quantify customer surplus with avoidable, incremental economics:

`incremental NPV = sum over actual payment dates t of [incremental contribution + avoidable cash costs saved - incremental product purchase/subscription/use/support costs - implementation/transition/replacement capital expenditure - incremental working capital + supported residual value/recovery] / (1+r)^t`

Measure every term relative to the best feasible alternative. Initial product purchases occur at time zero; prepaid subscriptions, later replacements and working-capital recovery retain their actual dates. Do not omit upfront equipment cost or count a residual twice. Increasing only the purchase price must reduce surplus and change adoption when its threshold is crossed.

Do not value the same saved labor hour both as labor removed and new output. Include quality, reliability, review/rework, latency and capacity only through supported consequences. Convert technical performance into cost per accepted job/output, not benchmark speed alone.

Separate three burdens: initial purchase/deployment; the incumbent customer's cost of leaving; and the new solution's future exit cost. Include file/data migration, interoperability, retraining, integration, contracts, certification, legal risk and dual running. Sunk expenditure is not a new switching cost. Segment existing renewals, new entrants, occasional users and regulated/enterprise workflows; incumbents can retain installed customers while losing new demand.

Model how switching costs themselves change: compatible formats, migration tools, AI automation, APIs, free tiers, bundling and distribution can erode or reinforce them. Customer benefit does not automatically accrue to the vendor: competition, multisourcing and buyer bargaining determine retained price and margin. Track willingness to pay, paid usage/renewals, churn, discounting, new-customer wins, unit support/inference costs and distribution charges.

For creative software, distinguish generation from editable production workflows, delivery formats, collaboration, asset libraries and compliance. Compare actual accepted outputs and total workflow cost for each cohort. A stronger rival generator may threaten new seats or price without immediately replacing an enterprise workflow. Bundling a rival model may defend distribution while increasing costs or weakening differentiated technology. Do not hard-code a conclusion about Adobe or any competitor from the user's example.

The future earnings path follows economically eligible AND funded AND qualified AND deployable customers, with cohort overlap counted once. Use observed cohorts or supported ranges; unknown private conversion rates become a threshold question, not an excuse to skip the future or fabricate precision.

The A09/A18 handoff to A07/A10 includes the cohort's reservation-price/payback bound, negotiated-price evidence, allocated qualified supply and necessary vendor investment. Raising price above the bound must change adoption or require a newly evidenced benefit. Do not assign an arbitrary fraction of customer surplus to the vendor or count an unfunded positive-NPV customer as a shipment.

## Forward state sheet at screening depth

Every advancing market needs an early state sheet for next quarter, next half-year, next year and normalized/full-cycle conditions. Record magnitudes/ranges where supported and direction/thresholds otherwise. Full finalists expand to the source's multi-resolution calendar; a two-month stock target never truncates the business model.

| State | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| Demand | End use/deployed systems × product content; replacement; inventory procurement; funded project timing; price-induced adoption or deferral |
| Supply | Existing good output, utilization, product allocation, process/yield, packaging/assembly, qualification, shared resources, entrant projects and delivery lead times |
| Inventory | Usable stock by owner/grade, desired stock, double orders, cancellations, customer advances and cost vintage |
| Prices/margins | Spot versus contract cohorts, comparable grades/mix, bargaining alternatives, renewal lag and recognized cost lag |
| Response | Customer substitution/redesign, supplier allocation/capex, financing, ramp/qualification and eventual normalization |
| Financials/beliefs | When physical change reaches shipments, accepted revenue, costs and cash; when investors can learn about future change |

Use `procurement = consumption + desired ending inventory - opening usable inventory`. Enforce `ending inventory = opening inventory + good supply - consumption` within the declared trade/flow boundary. An infeasible deficit requires rationing, substitution, delayed deployment or corrected inputs. Orders and backlog do not become consumption automatically.

Build demand and supply independently before reconciliation. Map shared capacity in common bottleneck hours or wafer equivalents with yield/content conversion. Do not assume HBM, conventional DRAM and NAND are interchangeable; do not sum wafers, bits, stacks and packages without conversion. Optical lanes, ports, modules, lasers, DSPs and fiber require a system topology and attachment map; capex growth is not module-volume growth. Product mix can tighten one grade while total industry output rises.

Separate structural change (content per system, replacement regime, architecture, capacity allocation) from temporary shocks (outage, expedited orders, inventory refill, freight, price reset). Each factor records: knowledge class, first-public date, economic start, exposed amount, duration/recovery, demand/supply/price channel, contract/cost/cash lags, rival response and falsifier. Use the knowledge classes in v2 section 20.4. Unannounced future shocks remain stress-only.

Test at least the most material opposing response: earlier qualified rival supply; substitution as prices rise; delayed customer funding; demand pulled forward; or yield improvements freeing capacity. A shortage does not identify a precise ASP or guarantee durable profit. Anticipate supply normalization before the peak reported quarter, and propagate expected normalization into customer ordering and valuation.

Before every forecast lock, A19/A23 perform a dated forward-factor sweep of launches, qualifications, funded expansions, customer budgets/cancellations, price/cost resets, replacement, financing, policy/legal changes and working-capital terms. Each material factor receives an included, excluded-with-reason, conflicted or unresolved disposition and dependency IDs. A later supply ramp still belongs in the model when it falls beyond the stock-price horizon.

## Turning-point card before detailed valuation

A14/A13 create an initial card during the comparable screen and refine it after the operating model. They must not alter the independent operating forecast to fit price.

1. **Define the underperformance.** Price versus which dates/benchmark, or operating results versus which comparison? Use the frozen forecast when one exists. On first coverage, use matched prior periods, relevant industry/customer changes or a declared descriptive baseline; do not invent a prior forecast. Diagnose demand, adoption/share, delivery, price/mix/cost, expenses, cash and valuation separately. Correct splits, dividends, FX and denominator changes. Do not assume a falling stock is mispriced.
2. **List competing explanations.** Deteriorating customer value/share; end-demand weakness; temporary inventory/capacity shock; rising capital needs/dilution; justified discount-rate change; expected future normalization; information/recognition lag; and positioning only where evidenced.
3. **State the conditional valuation requirements.** Several combinations of growth, margin, duration and required return can explain one price. Distinguish observed public narrative/guidance from inferred beliefs. Never say “the market expects X” from an arbitrary PE or lack of analyst coverage.
4. **Identify a discriminating leading observation.** Customer deployments, replenishment, realized contract prices, qualified output, renewal cohorts or rival results should separate the thesis from the strongest adverse explanation. Give its source, direction and break-even threshold.
5. **Connect three clocks.** Operating turn → measurement/disclosure → possible recognition before the price horizon. A future year can be repriced now; a merely scheduled earnings call is not evidence of a surprise.
6. **Specify what is new.** What is already public, what incremental evidence could arrive, which fixed-year earnings/duration belief it changes, and why existing pessimism may remain justified. State an event window, failure condition and revisit action if timing slips.

A CPU scarcity case, for example, needs server/client distinctions, customer demand, supplier allocations, rival qualified capacity, inventory, price/contract terms and fulfillment constraints. Test whether scarcity was knowable before the report and whether it was already communicated. Stronger financial results after publication do not prove an earlier earnings surprise was predictable or unpriced. Verify the actual issuer, quarter, date and price move before using a real event as an example.

After an outcome, retain the original forecast and split error into demand, share/adoption, supply, pricing/mix, cost, accounting/financing, timing and beliefs/valuation. If operations meet but price misses, the investment forecast still missed; do not declare the market irrational as a repair. A new version states which premise changed and which revisit route it opens.

## Financial and valuation discipline in the right place

Future earning capability can justify weak current cash or a high current multiple when customer value, funded adoption, supply, competitive duration and reinvestment support it. Model ramp costs, capital, working capital, financing/dilution and terminal competition explicitly. Current owner-cash yield is one diagnostic, not a discovery veto.

Use financial reconstruction to constrain and test the causal model. DCF and multiples are valuation lenses on that model, not two independent proofs when they share assumptions or one is calibrated to the other. Present future earnings requirements and normalized cash conversion at screening; use a justified fixed-year price bridge and discounted cash cross-check for the final judgment. Neither arbitrary multiples nor linear extrapolation of today's cash settle growth or turning-point cases.
